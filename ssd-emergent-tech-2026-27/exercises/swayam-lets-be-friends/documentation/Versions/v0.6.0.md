# v0.6.0 — Game Concept & Interaction Architecture

## Description

v0.6.0 expands the Evil Replica project from a face-tracking prototype into the foundation of the complete interactive horror game.

The previous version established the technical ability to detect the player's face and control the replica.

v0.6.0 defines how these systems will become part of the actual game experience.

The game begins as a friendly interaction between the player and the character.

The player is gradually introduced to camera interaction, physical movement, and eventually a face-controlled rhythm game.

As the game progresses, the replica begins to behave incorrectly.

The intended progression is:

NORMAL MOVEMENT
        ↓
SLOW MOVEMENT
        ↓
OPPOSITE MOVEMENT
        ↓
INDEPENDENT MOVEMENT
        ↓
HORROR SEQUENCE

The objective is to make the player initially believe that the replica is simply responding to them before revealing that it has become independent.

# Face-Controlled Rhythm Game

The next major gameplay system is based on the concept of a music-tile / rhythm game.

Instead of tapping tiles, arrows appear on the screen.

The player must move their face in the direction of the arrow at the correct moment to continue the song.

Example:

        ↑
        ↓
        ←
        →

The player physically moves their head according to the displayed direction.

The available directions are:

- LEFT
- RIGHT
- UP
- DOWN

The basic interaction is:

ARROW APPEARS
        ↓
PLAYER READS ARROW
        ↓
PLAYER MOVES FACE
        ↓
HEAD DIRECTION DETECTED
        ↓
TIMING CHECK
        ↓
CORRECT → SONG CONTINUES
INCORRECT → GAME RESPONSE

The existing head-tracking system provides the technical foundation for this interaction.

# Replica Movement Behaviour

The replica behaviour will change progressively during the rhythm-game experience.

The sequence is:

NORMAL
        ↓
SLOW
        ↓
OPPOSITE
        ↓
INDEPENDENT

These stages should not appear simultaneously.

They should be introduced gradually so that the player notices something is becoming wrong.

---

# Stage 1 — Normal Movement

Initially, the replica behaves like a normal reflection of the player.

PLAYER RIGHT → REPLICA RIGHT
PLAYER LEFT  → REPLICA LEFT
PLAYER UP    → REPLICA UP
PLAYER DOWN  → REPLICA DOWN

The purpose is to establish the player's expectation that the replica follows their movements.

---

# Stage 2 — Slow Movement

The replica begins responding more slowly.

The movement direction remains generally correct, but a delay is introduced.

The progression should be subtle:

NORMAL
   ↓
SLIGHT DELAY
   ↓
NOTICEABLE DELAY

The player should gradually realize that the replica is no longer perfectly synchronized.

---

# Stage 3 — Opposite Movement

The replica begins moving in the opposite direction to the player's movement.

PLAYER RIGHT → REPLICA LEFT
PLAYER LEFT  → REPLICA RIGHT

PLAYER UP    → REPLICA DOWN
PLAYER DOWN  → REPLICA UP

Head rotation / tilt can also be reversed.

This is the first major indication that the replica is behaving incorrectly.

---

# Stage 4 — Independent Movement

The replica eventually stops responding directly to the player's movement.

Instead, it begins moving according to its own behaviour.

At this point:

PLAYER MOVEMENT ≠ REPLICA MOVEMENT

The player is no longer controlling a copy of themselves.

The replica has become an independent entity.

This transition is extremely important to the horror experience.

---

# Final Horror Sequence

After the replica becomes independent, the final horror sequence begins.

## 1. Sudden Stop

The replica suddenly stops moving.

## 2. First Stare

The replica looks directly at the player.

The head has a slight downward tilt.

## 3. Black Eyes

The replica's eyes become completely black.

The replica continues staring at the player.

## 4. Chaotic Head Movement

The replica suddenly performs extremely fast, chaotic and unnatural head movements.

The intended feeling is supernatural and impossible rather than normal human movement.

The movement should feel similar in intensity to a supernatural / Doctor Strange-style chaotic head movement.

## 5. Sudden Stop

The chaotic movement stops abruptly.

## 6. Second Stare

The replica returns to:

- Slight downward head tilt.
- Completely black eyes.
- Direct stare at the player.

The stare should be held again.

## 7. Creepy Wide Smile

The replica slowly begins to smile.

The smile gradually becomes unnaturally wide and creepy.

## 8. Black Screen

The experience immediately transitions to a completely black screen.

The final sequence is:

STOP
 ↓
STARE
 ↓
DOWNWARD TILT
 ↓
BLACK EYES
 ↓
CHAOTIC FAST HEAD MOVEMENT
 ↓
STOP
 ↓
SECOND STARE
 ↓
CREEPY WIDE SMILE
 ↓
BLACK SCREEN

There is NO face-emergence stage.

# Replica System Architecture

The existing replica system should remain the foundation.

The architecture should follow:

CAMERA
   ↓
FACE / HAND DETECTION
   ↓
PLAYER INPUT DATA
   ↓
GAME STATE
   ↓
REPLICA BEHAVIOUR STATE
   ↓
BEHAVIOUR MODIFIER
   ↓
FINAL REPLICA MOVEMENT

The face-tracking system determines the player's movement.

The game state determines how the replica responds.

The behaviour modifier then converts the player's movement into the current replica behaviour.

Example:

FACE DATA
   ↓
BASE POSITION
   ↓
CURRENT BEHAVIOUR
   ↓
NORMAL / SLOW / OPPOSITE / INDEPENDENT
   ↓
FINAL REPLICA POSITION

This prevents different systems from fighting over the replica's position or transform.

---

# Important Development Rule

The current working face system must not be unnecessarily replaced.

When implementing new behaviour:

1. Preserve face tracking.
2. Preserve camera calibration.
3. Preserve face crop.
4. Preserve replica size.
5. Preserve replica alignment.
6. Add new behaviour as a separate layer.
7. Test the behaviour independently.
8. Connect it to gameplay only after it works correctly.

The existing face-replica foundation is considered stable.
